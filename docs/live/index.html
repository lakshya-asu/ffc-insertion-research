THE DESIGN DISCUSSION

The choices shaping this system.

Open a decision to see its rationale, the tradeoff and the evidence that would make us change it. These are working decisions, not a finished hardware specification.

01Start with two fixed cameras

Decision. Use a desk overview to find the cable and a board close-up to resolve the two ribbon sockets. The current renders use ideal 768 × 512 pinhole cameras.

Why. One overview does not give enough pixels to judge a tiny socket or an exposed cable end. Fixed mounts also make the first calibration and evaluation easier to understand.

Tradeoff. The close-up usually cannot see the loose cable, and neither camera yet represents a purchased lens or measured noise model.

Revisit when. Real images establish the required pixel scale and show where gripper occlusion prevents a usable tip estimate. That may justify another close-up or a wrist camera.

02Learn image regions before commanding motion

Decision. Train a small U-Net to segment cable body, contacts, stiffener, CSI and DSI. Keep the robot stationary.

Why. We can inspect the model’s errors directly and establish that it uses RGB before combining perception with actions.

Tradeoff. A region or centroid does not supply the physical end frame, face orientation, socket mouth or uncertainty needed for insertion.

Revisit when. Endpoint-specific estimates pass held-out real-camera checks against the task’s measured clearance. The next skill is bounded pickup, followed by tactile retention checks.

03Keep simulator truth outside runtime perception

Decision. Simulator labels are offline supervision and evaluation data. The inference container mounts RGB images, a frozen checkpoint and three code files.

Why. Those are inputs we can replace with real-camera images in the lab. Hidden cable or connector poses would invalidate that transfer.

Tradeoff. Ambiguous views must stay ambiguous. Later control needs uncertainty, touch and recovery instead of a simulator-state shortcut.

Evidence. The inference launcher has no label or USD mount, no network and no GPU device. A repeated isolated replay checks prediction stability. This does not yet qualify live synchronization.

04Improve the board with traceable geometry

Decision. Keep the community Pi 4 STEP and official Camera Module 3 STEP. Add 102 small package footprints recovered from the official board drawing, seven approximate labels and materials for existing connector solids.

Why. Board context helps distinguish the CSI camera socket from the similar DSI display socket. Small parts and labels provide visual structure missing from the first import.

Tradeoff. Package height/type and surface finishes remain assumptions. The extra detail does not prove photorealism.

Revisit when. Photos and measurements of the actual board revision reveal geometry, marking or reflectance mismatches. Inspect the before-and-after scene.

05Separate cable appearance from cable mechanics

Decision. Preserve drawing dimensions and opposite exposed faces while varying a static, length-preserving ribbon curve for perception.

Why. We can start testing appearance without claiming an unmeasured deformation model.

Tradeoff. The curve is authored; it has not settled under gravity. This dataset cannot validate bending, friction, buckling or grasp retention.

Revisit when. We measure laminate response, stiffener transitions and contact behavior. A reduced or FEA-informed model must reproduce held-out shapes and forces before contact learning.

06Freeze the model before opening the test set

Decision. Develop on 96 training scenes and 24 validation scenes, with two images per scene. Render 56 new evaluation scenes after checkpoint selection.

Why. Test failures should describe the frozen model rather than guide silent tuning on the benchmark.

Tradeoff. The same assets and camera placements appear across splits. These results measure synthetic scene generalization only.

Revisit when. We add new board/cable specimens and real images. The test protocol then needs explicit specimen and environment splits, larger failure sets and uncertainty calibration.

07Keep the phone connector as its own second task

Decision. Stage 1 is Pi ribbon insertion into an upright CSI socket. Stage 2 is a phone-style flex with a small connector pressed down onto the PCB.

Why. They share observation and robot infrastructure, but need different gripping surfaces, mating mechanics and completion checks.

Tradeoff. The second connector pair is not selected. Hirose BM28 is only a representative candidate, not a claimed phone part.

Revisit when. A matched plug/receptacle, handling specification and replaceable research coupon are chosen. Read the Stage 2 plan.

CURRENT SETUP / PROPOSED LAB HARDWARE

Which cameras, and where?

Today’s perception inputs are two simulated RGB cameras. No physical camera model has been purchased or calibrated in this experiment. There is no connected wrist camera, depth camera, tactile sensor or force transducer. The Camera Module 3 lying on the desk is the part we will connect to the Pi; it is not one of the observation cameras.

All use an ideal 36 × 24 mm image aperture. These virtual focal lengths are not lens purchase recommendations. Live previews of C1/C2 use 960 × 640; the recorded learning dataset uses 768 × 512. The board camera is about 19 cm above the PCB and views it about 36° away from its normal.

The overview is too coarse for contact metrology.

At the nominal cable position, its 1 mm contact pitch spans roughly 1.4 pixels and a 0.7 mm conductor is about one pixel wide. That estimate comes from the ideal camera geometry. We need higher pixel density and a close-up of the physical end before reliable edge/face estimation; enlarging this image after capture cannot restore the missing information.

NEXT MODEL COMPARISON

We should use stronger pretrained vision.

The U-Net is a reproducible first baseline, not a commitment to a small model for the finished robot. My preferred next comparison is a pretrained dense-feature backbone with task-specific segmentation and endpoint heads, alongside a promptable segmentation model for proposals and annotation assistance. Better performance on this task must be measured.

CandidateRole in this taskWhat still needs work
DINOv3 ViT-B/16 + dense task headsLearn CSI/DSI context, ribbon regions and end/edge features from a pretrained representation. Start with a frozen encoder, then compare limited fine-tuning.Its features need a decoder, high-resolution boundary refinement and physical endpoint supervision. Official checkpoints require approved access. Official repository

The 3.1 release adds improved multi-object tracking.Does not automatically provide the mating frame, contact face or calibrated clearance. This is an alternative to evaluate, not a claim that it already outperforms the baseline. Official models

Proposed inference sequence: find cable and board regions from RGB → inspect high-resolution end/socket crops → estimate physical edges, face and uncertainty → fuse tactile/force evidence during a later bounded skill. Crops must come from the model or a fixed calibrated view, not ground-truth coordinates.

We will compare per-class overlap, endpoint/orientation error, absent-object false positives, occlusion recovery, real-image performance, latency and peak GPU memory. Today’s held-out set is now visible, so model development must not quietly tune to it; use fresh evaluation scenes and a separate physical holdout. The stronger candidates are researched and specified here, but have not replaced the measured U-Net result.

What is still open

Real-camera validation, contact and latch geometry, calibrated cable mechanics, tactile sensing, reliable end-pose estimates and an uncertainty-aware action policy. No insertion result is claimed for this Pi scene.

How this live page works

A read-only service on the research machine publishes curated status and timestamped renderer snapshots. The page polls every two seconds. A paused renderer retains its last frame; the status explicitly changes to a saved frame as it ages. Dataset capture can publish new scene views as it runs.

The public connection uses a temporary Cloudflare Quick Tunnel. It works while the machine and tunnel service are running; the hostname can change after a tunnel restart. The GitHub page remains the stable entry point. No terminal, arbitrary files or robot command endpoint is exposed.
